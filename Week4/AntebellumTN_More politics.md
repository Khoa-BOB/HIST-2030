## Summary

### Lecture 7, Part 3: The Whig–Democrat Rivalry, Polk & the Volunteer State — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### Whig–Democrat Party Rivalry
* **Whig Party vs. Democratic Party:** The dominant back-and-forth rivalry structuring Tennessee politics from the late 1830s through the 1850s, marked by high voter turnout, energetic public campaigning, and formal party conventions.
* **1839 Governor's Race:** Democrat **James K. Polk** defeats incumbent Whig **Newton Cannon** with 51% of the vote, reviving the Tennessee Democratic Party from near-extinction.
* **Sarah Polk:** James Polk's wife, who ran his campaign headquarters and correspondence, and later — as First Lady — closely managed White House affairs and current events for him.

##### Polk's Political Defeats & the 1844 Election
* **James C. "Lean Jimmy" Jones:** Charming, anecdote-telling Whig candidate who defeated Polk in both the 1841 and 1843 Tennessee governor's races.
* **1844 Presidential Election:** Despite losing his home state's governorship twice, Polk becomes the Democrats' pro-expansion presidential nominee after Martin Van Buren opposes Texas annexation; Polk wins the presidency nationally even as Whig Henry Clay carries Tennessee.

##### The Mexican-American War & the "Volunteer State"
* **Mexican-American War:** The defining issue of Polk's presidency; Tennessee's massive over-volunteering (legend holds ~30,000 responded to a call for 2,800 men) is popularly credited with the "Volunteer State" nickname.
* **War of 1812 Origins:** The lecture argues Tennessee's volunteer reputation actually originated earlier, during the War of 1812, and was merely reinforced by the Mexican-American War.

##### Late 1840s Governors' Races
* **Aaron V. Brown (1845):** Democratic candidate who wins the 1845 governor's race against Whig **Ephraim H. Foster**, reflecting Tennessee's pro-annexation, pro-expansion mood.
* **Neill S. Brown (1847):** Whig candidate who wins the 1847 Tennessee governor's race.
* **Zachary Taylor (1848):** Whig candidate who wins the presidency in 1848.
* **William Trousdale (1849):** Democratic candidate who closes out the decade by winning the 1849 governor's race.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Personality-driven politics:** Polk's repeated losses to the charismatic "Lean Jimmy" Jones show that in this era, personal charm and campaign style could outweigh party positioning in statewide races.
2. **Women's informal political influence:** Sarah Polk's active, if unelected, role in her husband's campaigns and presidency illustrates how Tennessee women exercised real political influence despite being denied the vote.
3. **Myth vs. mechanism in state identity:** The "Volunteer State" nickname is popularly attributed to the Mexican-American War, but the lecture traces its actual roots to War of 1812-era volunteering — a reminder to distinguish popular legend from historical origin.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This lecture closes out Tennessee's antebellum political history by tracing the Whig–Democrat rivalry through the 1840s and the rise of James K. Polk to the presidency.

1. **The Whig–Democrat Rivalry (late 1830s–1850s):** Tennessee politics in this period was dominated by an energetic, high-turnout back-and-forth between the **Whig Party** and the **Democratic Party**, featuring public rallies, formal conventions, and popular campaigning descended from the style of the Jackson era.
2. **Polk's Rise and Repeated Defeats (1839–1843):** **James K. Polk** won the 1839 governor's race with 51% of the vote, reviving the state's Democratic Party, aided significantly by his wife **Sarah Polk**'s campaign work. However, Polk then lost the governorship twice, in 1841 and 1843, to the charismatic Whig **James "Lean Jimmy" Jones**.
3. **The 1844 Presidential Election:** Despite his home-state setbacks, the national Democratic Party nominated Polk in 1844 as a pro-expansion candidate after Martin Van Buren's opposition to Texas annexation cost him the nomination. Polk won the presidency nationally even though Whig candidate **Henry Clay** carried Tennessee — a stark irony for a "favorite son."
4. **The Mexican-American War and the "Volunteer State":** The Mexican-American War became the defining issue of Polk's presidency. Tennessee's outsized response to the governor's call for volunteers (legend says roughly 30,000 responded to a request for 2,800) cemented the state's "Volunteer State" nickname — though the lecture notes the reputation actually traces back further, to Tennessee's volunteer militias in the **War of 1812**.
5. **Late 1840s Governors' Races:** The Whig–Democrat back-and-forth continued through the decade's end: Democrat **Aaron V. Brown** defeated Whig **Ephraim H. Foster** in 1845; Whig **Neill S. Brown** won in 1847 (the same year Whig **Zachary Taylor** won the presidency); and Democrat **William Trousdale** closed out the decade with a win in 1849 — leaving the rivalry very much alive heading into the 1850s.

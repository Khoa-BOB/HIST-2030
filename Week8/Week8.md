# Week 8 Summary & Study Focus

**Big picture:** Week 8 covers Reconstruction in Tennessee. It runs from the 1865 Union Convention, through Andrew Johnson's presidency, the Memphis Massacre, and Brownlow's Radical Republican rule, to the Ku Klux Klan's founding in Pulaski. It ends with the conservative "redemption" that produced the Constitution of 1870.

### Quick recap
- **Lecture 32 – Reconstruction 101:** Reconstruction (c. 1865–1877) was the political rebuilding of the nation and, following **Eric Foner**, its adjustment to the end of slavery. Tennessee started early because of Union occupation (1862–63) and was the first state to rejoin. The three amendments: **13th** (abolition), **14th** (citizenship, due process, equal protection), **15th** (1870, no racial bar on voting, but sex not covered). Read Democrats and Republicans in their own historical context.
- **Lecture 33 – Reconstruction in TN, pt. I:** The **Union Convention (Nashville, Jan 1865)**, made up of Unionists, soldiers, and radicals, abolished slavery in the state constitution, repudiated secession, nullified legislative acts after May 1861, and limited voting to men eligible in 1864. The **petition of Nashville's Black residents** claimed freedom as a natural right and asked for citizenship, citing Union army service and the pre-1835 precedent of free men of color voting. **Brownlow** became the sole candidate for governor, the 13th Amendment was ratified in April 1865, and readmission came in 1866.
- **Lecture 34 – Reconstruction, pt. II:** **Andrew Johnson** was lenient to ex-Confederates and vetoed civil rights bills, and he was impeached but not removed. The **Memphis Massacre (Apr 30–May 3, 1866)** killed at least 46 African Americans. Congress passed the **Civil Rights Act of 1866**, the 14th Amendment, and the **Military Reconstruction Acts**, which did not apply to Tennessee, so there was no federal army there. **Brownlow** disfranchised Confederates (1865, 1866 laws), extended civil rights (Jan 1866), and gave **Black men the vote in 1867**. Tennessee rejected the 15th Amendment. Freed people built businesses, Fisk (1866), and churches, and won local offices.
- **Lecture 35 – Backlash & the Early Klan:** The **KKK** was founded in **Pulaski (1866)** by six young ex-Confederates. **Elaine Frantz Parsons** sees non-political origins that quickly turned into paramilitary terror, peaking from spring 1868 to mid-1871 and targeting Black people and white Republicans. **Forrest** became Grand Wizard in 1867. A 1868 anti-Klan law, the State Guard, and federal prosecutions crushed the organized Klan, but **Senter** loosened the franchise in 1869, producing a conservative landslide. The **Constitution of 1870** (convention chaired by **John C. Brown**, an ex-Confederate general and Klansman who became governor) brought universal male suffrage plus a **poll tax**.

### What to focus on
1. **Tennessee's unique Reconstruction:** It started early through occupation, was readmitted first (1866) by ratifying the 14th Amendment, was exempt from military reconstruction (so had no federal troops), and had a strong Unionist base that let Radicals govern. This continues the "last to leave, first to return" theme from Week 6.
2. **The three amendments:** Know what each one does and its limits (the 15th excludes sex and was later evaded by "colorblind" rules like the poll tax). Tennessee gave Black men the vote in 1867 but refused to ratify the 15th until the late 20th century.
3. **Johnson vs. Congress:** Johnson's leniency and vetoes, plus the Memphis Massacre, pushed Congress to take over Reconstruction.
4. **Opportunity vs. backlash:** Black voting, officeholding, businesses, Fisk, and the Freedmen's Bureau, set against Confederate disfranchisement, Klan terror, and the 1869–70 redemption.
5. **The Klan's evolution (Parsons):** From a Pulaski "social club" to a regional terrorist network tied to the Democratic Party. Its appeal came from defeat, disfranchisement, and lost patriarchal power.
6. **Anchor dates:** 1862–63 (Union occupation), Jan 1865 (Union Convention), Apr 1865 (Lincoln assassinated; TN ratifies 13th), June 1865 (franchise law), 1866 (Civil Rights Act; TN civil rights law; Memphis Massacre; Fisk; KKK founded; TN readmitted), 1867 (Black male suffrage in TN; Brownlow re-elected; Forrest Grand Wizard), 1868 (anti-Klan law; Black officeholding; Grant elected), 1869 (Senter wins; conservative landslide), 1870 (constitutional convention; Constitution of 1870; John C. Brown governor; 15th Amendment ratified).

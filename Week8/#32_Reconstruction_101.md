## Summary

### Lecture 32: Reconstruction 101 — Definitions, Amendments & Political Parties — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### Defining Reconstruction
* **Reconstruction (c. 1865–1877):** The period after the American Civil War in which the nation was reunified and the South rebuilt politically. This course looks at it mainly through its **politics**.
* **Tennessee's Early Start:** **Union occupation** spread across West, Middle, and finally East Tennessee in **1862–1863**, so Reconstruction began earlier in Tennessee than almost anywhere else. **Louisiana** is the other exception. Tennessee was the last state to leave the Union and the **first to rejoin**.
* **Eric Foner's Framing:** Paraphrasing historian **Eric Foner**, Reconstruction was also the nation's **adjustment to the end of slavery**. Freed people moved from enslavement to freedom, and everyone else had to adjust to that freedom. More than **200,000** people were freed in Tennessee and millions across the South.

##### The Reconstruction Amendments
* **13th Amendment:** Abolished slavery.
* **14th Amendment (Section 1):** Makes all persons **born or naturalized** in the U.S. citizens. It bars any state from abridging the **privileges or immunities** of citizens or denying **due process** or **equal protection of the laws**. It was meant mainly to extend citizenship rights to freed people and other people of color who had not been guaranteed them, and no state (including Tennessee) could abridge those rights.
* **15th Amendment (ratified 1870):** Prohibits denying the vote based on **race, color, or previous condition of servitude**. It does **not** cover sex, so women could still be denied the vote. African American men in some places voted even before it was ratified.

##### Politics & Historical Thinking
* **"Colorblind" Voter Restrictions:** Southern states later found ways around the 15th Amendment with rules that looked race-neutral on paper but were meant to keep Black men and women out of politics.
* **Democrats & Republicans:** The two major parties of the era. Students must understand them **in the context of their own time** and not project today's party associations onto them.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Occupation sets the clock:** Early and complete Union occupation is why Tennessee started Reconstruction ahead of most of the South and was the first state readmitted.
2. **From abolition to citizenship to suffrage:** The three amendments build on each other: ending slavery (13th), defining citizenship and its protections (14th), and protecting the vote from racial bars (15th).
3. **Formal rights vs. practical evasion:** The amendments set constitutional guarantees, but Southern states soon used seemingly race-neutral voting rules to get around them.
4. **Think historically:** Party labels meant something different in the 1860s, so the lecture warns against reading present-day politics back into Reconstruction.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This introductory lecture defines Reconstruction, explains why Tennessee's Reconstruction was unusual, and lays out the constitutional and political vocabulary needed for the rest of the unit.

1. **What Reconstruction Was (1865–1877):** **Reconstruction** is usually dated **1865–1877**, but Tennessee is special. Because the **Union army** occupied the whole state during **1862–1863**, Reconstruction began there earlier than elsewhere (except **Louisiana**). Tennessee was the last state to secede and the **first to rejoin** the Union. Following **Eric Foner**, the lecturer also frames Reconstruction as the nation adjusting to the **end of slavery**, as more than **200,000** freed people in Tennessee worked out what their freedom would mean.
2. **The Three Reconstruction Amendments:** The **13th Amendment** abolished slavery. The **14th Amendment** made everyone born or naturalized in the U.S. a citizen and barred states from abridging citizens' **privileges or immunities** or denying **due process** and **equal protection**. Above all, it was meant to extend citizenship to freed people and other people of color. The **15th Amendment (1870)** banned denying the vote because of **race, color, or previous servitude**, but it left out sex, so women could still be excluded.
3. **How the Amendments Played Out:** The 15th Amendment guaranteed that no state could explicitly bar Black men from voting. Southern states, however, would develop **seemingly colorblind voting restrictions** meant to limit Black political participation, a pattern to watch as the course moves forward.
4. **Reading the Parties Historically:** The unit centers on the **Democrats** and **Republicans**. The lecturer stresses understanding these parties as they worked in the 1860s and 1870s, without importing modern assumptions.

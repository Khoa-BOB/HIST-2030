# Southern Slave-holding; and Northern Slave-importing.

Which is worst, to tolerate the existence of a system of servitude, inherited from our fathers, with the soil on which we were born; or to be actively engaged in the traffic, which produced this servitude? Look at the following statements:—

Extract of a letter from Samuel Hodges, jr. American consul at the Cape de Verds, dated St. Jago, March 23.

“Captured by his Britannic majesty’s brig of war Thistle, capt. Hagan, brig Rambler, Churchill, of Bristol, Rhode Island, and schr. ——, Taylor, (late Lawton) of do; also, another schooner, an American, name unknown, with a full cargo of slaves, ordered for Sierra Leone.”

From the New-York Columbian.

Disgraceful!—It is reported that some of the slave-vessels, lately captured by the Cyane while engaged in that most inhuman and unlawful traffic, are owned in this city;—by very respectable merchants!—men professing the highest regard, not only for the laws of their country, but for the Laws of God! Alas, poor human nature.

Add to these facts, that Judge Story, the other day, in addressing the Grand Jury from the Bench, was obliged to lament that his New-England brethren, had been largely and actively engaged in this most unchristian traffic; and specially to direct the attention of the Grand Jury to the subject.

Yet in the face of all these facts, our pharisaical brethren can pronounce the “stand off—we are holier than ye.”—They can bellow and rave in favor of Missouri restriction, in public, and, in private employ their wealth and enterprise in this unrighteous business.—“Cast first the beam out thine own eye.”—Balt. Patriot.

A few words are difficult to read because of the old print quality, especially the ship/person names, but the main argument is very clear: the article is criticizing Northern merchants for participating in the slave trade while simultaneously condemning Southern slaveholding and supporting restrictions on Missouri.

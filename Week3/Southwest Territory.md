## Summary

### Lecture 5, Part 1: The Southwest Territory — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### From Franklin's Collapse to a Federal Territory
* **North Carolina Ratifies the U.S. Constitution:** North Carolina's ratification set up its second (and final) cession of its western lands, this time to the new federal government rather than the Confederation.
* **Southwest Territory (Territory South of the River Ohio):** Established October 1790 out of North Carolina's ceded western lands, with its capital initially at Rocky Mount; organized around the counties already established in the region.
* **William Blount:** Appointed territorial governor, responsible for making appointments and administering the territory; also negotiated directly with the Cherokee and worked closely with John Sevier.
* **John Sevier:** Former governor of the failed State of Franklin, who agreed to work with Blount's territorial administration.

##### Land Conflict & the Treaty of Holston (1791)
* **Ongoing Cherokee-Settler Conflict:** White settlers continued streaming into, and squatting on, land still legally reserved to the Cherokee, particularly south of the French Broad River — a recurring source of violence Blount had to manage.
* **Treaty of Holston (1791):** A treaty negotiated with the Cherokee that legitimized settlement south of the French Broad River, formally opening land that had previously been off-limits, even though it did not end tensions with the Cherokee and Creek.

##### Building Toward Statehood
* **1790 Territorial Census:** Found roughly 35,000 residents in the Southwest Territory (about 29,000 in the Washington District alone), including about 3,400 enslaved people, and about 3,600 people illegally settled south of the French Broad — short of the 5,000 adult men needed under the Northwest Ordinance's framework to form a territorial legislature.
* **Relocation to Knoxville:** Blount moved the territorial capital from Rocky Mount to the newly founded town of Knoxville, named for Secretary of War Henry Knox.
* **1795 Census & Statehood Referendum:** A second census (ordered by the new territorial legislature) found the territory had grown enough to potentially qualify for statehood (the Northwest Ordinance framework required 60,000 free inhabitants); an accompanying referendum saw an overwhelming majority vote in favor of pursuing statehood.
* **1796 Constitutional Convention:** Roughly 55 delegates convened in Knoxville in January 1796, drafted a state constitution, and sent it to Congress via President Washington — setting up Tennessee's admission as the 16th state.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **From Failed State to Federal Territory:** Franklin's collapse didn't end Tennessee's push for self-government — it simply redirected it through the proper federal channel once North Carolina ceded the same land to the U.S. government instead of trying to hold it directly.
2. **Land Cession as the Engine of Growth:** Just as with Sycamore Shoals and the earlier Overmountain treaties, the Treaty of Holston's legalization of previously "illegal" settlements is what allowed the territory's population — and its case for eventual statehood — to grow.
3. **Population Thresholds as the Statehood Gate:** The entire administrative arc of the territory (census counts, the fight to form a legislature, the 1795 referendum) was structured around hitting specific population benchmarks borrowed from the Northwest Ordinance model, showing how mechanical (not just political) the path to statehood actually was.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This lecture picks up right after the collapse of the State of Franklin, tracing how the same region became the federally organized Southwest Territory and built its case for statehood.

1. **Founding the Southwest Territory (1790):** After North Carolina ratified the U.S. Constitution and ceded its western lands to the new federal government, the **Southwest Territory** was established in October 1790, with **William Blount** appointed territorial governor and Rocky Mount as its initial capital. Blount worked with **John Sevier** and immediately faced the same land conflicts that had plagued Franklin — white settlers squatting on land still reserved to the Cherokee, especially south of the French Broad River.
2. **The Treaty of Holston (1791):** To ease this conflict, Blount negotiated the **Treaty of Holston** with the Cherokee in 1791, formally legitimizing settlement south of the French Broad River. The treaty opened new land to settlement but did not fully resolve tensions, which continued to flare with the Cherokee and Creek through the mid-1790s.
3. **Growth and the Push for a Legislature:** Blount relocated the territorial capital to the newly founded town of **Knoxville** (named for Secretary of War Henry Knox). A 1790 census found about 35,000 residents — short of the population needed to form a territorial legislature — but continued growth soon cleared that bar.
4. **The 1795 Census, Referendum, and Constitutional Convention:** A second census in 1795 showed the territory had grown enough to plausibly meet the Northwest Ordinance's 60,000-person threshold for statehood, and an accompanying referendum saw an overwhelming majority of participants vote in favor of pursuing statehood. This led directly to a **constitutional convention** in Knoxville in January 1796, where roughly 55 delegates drafted a state constitution and sent it to Congress through President Washington — setting the stage for Tennessee's admission as the 16th state.

# Week 1 Summary & Study Focus

**Big picture:** Week 1 moves from Tennessee's basic geography into "deep time" — the roughly 13,000-year human history of the region before it was ever "Tennessee." The two First Tennesseans lectures trace Native societies from the earliest hunters through the arrival of Europeans and the upheaval that followed, ending right at the doorstep of the historic tribal nations (Cherokee, Chickasaw, Creek, Choctaw) that the rest of the course will keep coming back to.

### Quick recap
- **Lecture 1 – Tennessee History & Deep Time:** Orienting facts — the state's dimensions, its three major rivers (Cumberland, Mississippi, Tennessee), and its limestone geology. Also flags a key methods point: historians rely on written records, which is why everything before European contact counts as "pre-history" and has to be reconstructed from archaeology instead.
- **Lecture 2 – The First Tennesseans, pt. 1:** The long cultural sequence of Native life in Tennessee — Paleo-Indian → Archaic → Woodland → Mississippian (~13,000 BP to ~1600 CE) — capped by the arrival of Hernando de Soto in 1540, the first major European contact.
- **Lecture 3 – The First Tennesseans, pt. 2:** What de Soto's arrival set in motion — the "Mississippian Shatter Zone" (historian Robbie Ethridge's term), roughly 1540–1715, when disease, trade dependency, and the Indian slave trade destroyed the small pre-contact chiefdoms and forced survivors to coalesce into the large historic nations that would dominate the 18th-century South.

### What to focus on
1. **The four-stage cultural sequence** (Paleo-Indian → Archaic → Woodland → Mississippian) and roughly what changes at each stage — mobility, subsistence, settlement size, social complexity. This is the backbone Lecture 2 is built on.
2. **The Shatter Zone concept** — this is the week's central analytical idea, not just a list of bad things that happened. Be able to explain its *causes* (disease, the guns-for-slaves trade cycle, warfare, incorporation into a European-dominated economy) and its *outcome* (coalescence).
3. **Coalescence** — why and how small, decentralized Mississippian polities consolidated into the Cherokee, Chickasaw, Creek, and Choctaw by the 1700s. This is the throughline connecting pt. 1 and pt. 2.
4. **Anchor dates** — 1540 (de Soto / shatter zone begins), 1696 (smallpox outbreak), 1715 (Yamasee War, shatter zone ends, "Colonial South" emerges). These three dates are enough to place everything else in sequence.
5. **The Cherokee's specific arc** — initially avoided the slave trade, then armed their own slave raiders around 1700, then emerged as the dominant Native power allied with the English. Worth knowing well since the Cherokee recur throughout the rest of the course.

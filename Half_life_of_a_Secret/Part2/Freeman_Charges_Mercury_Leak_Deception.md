# Freeman Charges Mercury Leak Deception

> "I just think there's been a damned cover-up up there at Oak Ridge. Somebody ought to get to the bottom of it."
> — S. David Freeman

**United Press International**
*The Tennessean*, Front Page (p. 1) — Sunday, June 5, 1983

---

OAK RIDGE, Tenn. (UPI) — TVA Director S. David Freeman charged yesterday that the U.S. Department of Energy tried to "cover up" mercury contamination at a top-secret nuclear weapons plant by misleading TVA about its seriousness.

"I think the Department of Energy has withheld information from our people beginning back in 1977, and we don't have that information yet," Freeman said.

**"I JUST** think there's been a damned cover-up up there at Oak Ridge. Somebody ought to get to the bottom of it."

The Energy Research and Development Administration, now the Department of Energy, met with TVA scientists in early 1977 to get expert opinion on data it had gathered about mercury contamination near the Y-12 nuclear weapons plant.

Ralph Brooks, assistant director of TVA's Division of Air and Water Resources, said TVA's experts concluded from the data that mercury pollution of a creek near the plant did not present a human health hazard.

Therefore, TVA denied the energy agency's request that Poplar Creek be added to TVA's ongoing mercury monitoring program at the agency's reservoirs.

**DOE DISCLOSED** last month that it had lost into the "air, ground or water" at Y-12 an estimated 2.4 million pounds of mercury between 1950 and 1977. Officials said about 475,000 pounds of the toxic liquid metal wound up in the East Fork Poplar Creek, which flows through Oak Ridge and empties into the Clinch River.

Brooks said yesterday he now believes DOE withheld documents at the 1977 meeting that indicated high levels of mercury poisoning in waters downstream from the plant. He said DOE apparently misled state health authorities also.

**JERRY WING,** chief of DOE's environmental protection branch in Oak Ridge, said yesterday he was not familiar with the specific charges lodged by TVA officials but insisted, "We never tried to mislead anybody."

The state Public Health Department posted signs along East Fork Poplar Creek last fall warning that it could be unhealthy to eat fish caught from the creek. The signs were posted after DOE provided the state with more complete information about the extent of the pollution.

TVA Chairman Charles Dean sent a letter to DOE Secretary Donald Hodel Friday asking him to "do everything within your power to provide the state of Tennessee and TVA" with complete information about the mercury problem.

"We further urge you to disclose any information your department has about any other toxic or hazardous materials may have escaped into the environment at Oak Ridge," Dean wrote.

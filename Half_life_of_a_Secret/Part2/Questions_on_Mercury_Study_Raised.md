# Questions on mercury study raised

### Oak Ridge residents still unconvinced levels safe

**Associated Press**
*The Tennessean*, p. 3-B — Thursday, November 14, 1985

---

OAK RIDGE (AP) — Citizens living downstream from a nuclear weapons plant say they are unconvinced by a federal-state study showing their health was not at risk despite years of mercury pollution.

"I don't believe none of it. We get these reports and we don't understand what in the world is going on," said Paul White, head of the citizens' group that pushed for the two-year study.

"Your report says there is no substantial hazard. Yet signs remain that say fishing is prohibited," said Al Brooks, who lives in the East Fork Poplar Creek area where the worst mercury contamination occurred.

The study, released Tuesday, was begun after a public furor arose over the Department of Energy's admission in 1983 that it could not account for 2.4 million pounds of mercury over the past 30 years.

The Energy Department later said as much as 470,000 pounds of the toxic metal may have been released into East Fork Poplar Creek, which flows from the Y-12 weapons plant through Oak Ridge.

"We have good news for the people of Oak Ridge," said Dr. Michael Bruner, assistant commissioner of the Tennessee Department of Health and Environment. "Our study has concluded that the people have no additional health risk."

Bruner called the study a "first step" in cleaning up Oak Ridge's "massive environmental problems."

Dr. Diane Rowley, an epidemiologist with the Centers for Disease Control in Atlanta, which helped conduct the study, said future health studies in Oak Ridge should not be limited to a single contaminant.

The state-federal survey consisted of hair and urine samples and interviews with 2,627 residents, or about 10% of the Oak Ridge population. Half of those surveyed live in areas of possible mercury contamination. The rest served as a control group for the study.

The health study concluded that the mercury levels in bodies of people exposed to the mercury were no greater than mercury levels which occur normally.

In addition to the Y-12 weapons plant, Oak Ridge is the site of the Oak Ridge National Laboratory and a gaseous diffusion plant where uranium is enriched for fuel and weapons purposes.

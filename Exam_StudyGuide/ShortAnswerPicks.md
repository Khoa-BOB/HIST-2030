### 1. Tennessee's Prehistoric Past
**Source:** `Week1/#1 Tennessee History & Deep time.md`

| Part of the question | Evidence from the lecture |
|---|---|
| Why prehistory matters | Prehistory is "before written records are available." The lecturer argues "human history and material history are not all that separate." |
| Non-human species | Paleozoic marine creatures; Mesozoic "clams, oysters, and snails"; prehistoric "elephants, alligators"; mastodon remains; at the Gray site, "alligators, mastodons, rhinos," a red panda, and a new rhino species ("Little Guy") |
| Major environmental changes | The land formed underwater about 1 billion years ago. A Paleozoic shallow sea and swampy deltas produced the coal deposits, and most of the state was "above sea level" by the end of the Paleozoic. West Tennessee stayed underwater into the Mesozoic. The climate was cooler during the Ice Age, "even if the ice sheets did not reach Tennessee." |
| Why we're still learning | The Gray Fossil Site was found in 2000 during a road project. Its 200+ species include some "entirely unique in the Americas," and the lecture says "we're constantly finding new records." |

### 2. New Madrid Earthquakes
**Source:** `Week4/NewMaridEarthquaks+SecondGreatAwakening in TN.md`

This question asks only one thing, "why spiritual significance?", and the lecture answers it from two sides:
- **White settlers:** a "terrifying roar of underground thunder that represented the threat of damnation to fearful Christians," "the devil smells of burning sulfur," and "Is this God Himself, shaking the land, his hellfire…?" The lecture ties this to the Second Great Awakening, where revival preaching moved people to physical shaking, which matched the shaking earth.
- **Native peoples:** historian Conevery Bolton Valencius calls the quakes "powerful spiritual signs." For the Cherokee they brought a "wave of prophetic" fervor tied to resisting "Americanization" and "dislocation." Some Cherokees saw the end of the world. Others saw "a symbol of anger from the Great Spirit directed at white settlers." The lecture links this to Tecumseh's movement.

### Lecture 4, Part 3: The Frontier — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### Land Speculation & the Transylvania Purchase (1775)
* **Richard Henderson & Company:** North Carolina judge who, in spring 1775, formed a land-speculating company (historically known as the Transylvania Company) with associates to negotiate a massive land purchase from the Cherokee.
* **Treaty of Sycamore Shoals (March 1775):** Agreement negotiated without sanction from British or colonial authorities, in which the Cherokee relinquished claims to land between the Ohio/Kentucky Rivers south to the ridge dividing the Tennessee and Cumberland Rivers — opening the way for settlement of Middle Tennessee.
* **Cherokee Opposition to the Purchase:** A faction of Cherokee leaders (historically associated with **Dragging Canoe**) disagreed with the sale, viewing it as certain to invite further white encroachment.
* **Cherokee Support for the Purchase:** Other Cherokee leaders — the transcript names one only phonetically, most likely **Oconostota** — considered the deal advantageous, since it exchanged goods, ammunition, and money for claims to land the Cherokee viewed as already contested by other Indigenous groups.

##### Revolutionary War Disruption & Cherokee-Settler Conflict (1776)
* **Outbreak of the American Revolutionary War:** Disrupted colonial and British authority in the southern backcountry, which encouraged squatters and speculators to grab available land while oversight was weak.
* **British Agents' Policy Reversal (1776):** British Indian agents initially urged the Cherokee toward neutrality, then reversed course in 1776 to encourage Cherokee action against Patriot-aligned settlers.
* **May 1776 Ultimatum:** British officials demanded that Overmountain settlers vacate their homes, relocating either north of the Donelson Line or south into British Florida; the settlers refused and instead built new defenses.
* **Three-Pronged Cherokee Offensive (July 1776):** Increasingly hostile Cherokee forces launched a coordinated, three-pronged attack on white settlements in upper East Tennessee, prompting neighboring colonies to send militia support.

##### Peace Treaties & Legitimized Settlements (1777)
* **1777 Peace Negotiations:** Following the 1776 conflict, Virginia (under Governor **Patrick Henry**) and North Carolina sent special commissioners to negotiate separate peace treaties with the Cherokee, signed in summer 1777.
* **Formal Legitimization of the Four Settlements:** The 1777 treaties formally ceded land under new boundaries, legally recognizing all four Overmountain settlements (North Holston, Watauga, Carter's Valley, and Nolichucky) despite both sides eventually breaking these agreements within a few years.

##### The Overmountain Men & the Battle of Kings Mountain (1780)
* **Weakening of Cherokee Regional Authority:** The disruptions of the 1770s significantly weakened Cherokee control of the region, encouraging further settler migration into Tennessee.
* **Overmountain Muster (September 1780):** Roughly 900 men gathered at a mustering point in the Watauga region before marching to intercept British forces in October 1780.
* **Colonel John Sevier:** Overmountain leader who led the attack at the Battle of Kings Mountain, emerging from the battle as a heroic frontier figure.
* **Battle of Kings Mountain (October 1780):** A decisive Patriot victory that forced the surrender of British-aligned Loyalist forces and is considered a major turning point in the southern theater of the American Revolutionary War.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Speculation Outpaces Sanction:** **Richard Henderson's** unauthorized **Treaty of Sycamore Shoals** shows settlers and speculators acting independently of British or colonial authority to acquire Cherokee land — the same pattern already seen in the Watauga Association's independent lease negotiations.
2. **War as a Catalyst for Encroachment:** The outbreak of the **Revolutionary War** disrupted colonial and British oversight of the backcountry, and speculators and squatters exploited that vacuum to accelerate settlement even as it also provoked violent Cherokee resistance.
3. **Alliance Politics Cut Both Ways:** British agents' 1776 reversal — from urging Cherokee neutrality to encouraging attacks on Patriot settlers — pulled the Cherokee directly into the Revolutionary War, provoking retaliation and treaties that cost them still more land and authority in 1777.
4. **From Contested Purchase to Legitimized Settlement:** Despite originating in a legally dubious land deal and escalating into open warfare, all four Overmountain settlements ended the decade formally legalized, and the resulting collapse of Cherokee regional authority fueled the population growth that produced the **Overmountain Men** of 1780.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This lecture traces Tennessee's frontier through the mid-to-late 1770s, from a controversial land purchase through Revolutionary War-era conflict to a celebrated Patriot victory:

1. **The Transylvania Purchase (Spring 1775):** In March 1775, **Richard Henderson**, a North Carolina judge, led a land-speculating company in negotiating the **Treaty of Sycamore Shoals** with the Cherokee — an unsanctioned deal ceding a vast tract between the Ohio/Kentucky Rivers and the Tennessee-Cumberland divide. Cherokee leaders were divided: some opposed the sale as an invitation to further encroachment, while others accepted it as a fair exchange of goods and money for land whose Cherokee claim was itself contested by other Indigenous groups. The purchase opened the way for settlement of Middle Tennessee.
2. **Revolutionary War Disruption (1776):** The outbreak of the **American Revolutionary War** weakened colonial and British authority in the southern backcountry, emboldening squatters and speculators to grab land while oversight was distracted. British Indian agents, who had initially urged Cherokee neutrality, reversed course in 1776 and encouraged the Cherokee to move against Patriot-aligned settlers, who in turn refused a British ultimatum to abandon their homes.
3. **Cherokee-Settler War and the 1777 Peace Treaties:** Cherokee forces launched a **three-pronged offensive** against Overmountain settlements in July 1776, drawing military support from neighboring colonies. The conflict was resolved the following year when Virginia (under Governor **Patrick Henry**) and North Carolina sent commissioners to negotiate separate peace treaties, signed in summer 1777, which formally ceded land under new boundaries and legally recognized all four Overmountain settlements — North Holston, Watauga, Carter's Valley, and Nolichucky.
4. **The Overmountain Men and Kings Mountain (1780):** The upheavals of the 1770s severely weakened Cherokee authority in the region, drawing in more settlers. In September 1780, roughly 900 Overmountain men mustered before marching to confront British forces, and in October 1780 — under leaders including **Colonel John Sevier** — they won a decisive victory at the **Battle of Kings Mountain**, a major turning point in the Revolutionary War's southern theater that cemented Sevier's reputation as a frontier hero.

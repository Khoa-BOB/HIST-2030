## Summary

### Lecture 22: The Road to Disunion — The 1850s & Tennessee History — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### Party Politics in 1850s Tennessee
* **Collapse of the Whig Party:** The Whig–Democrat rivalry that defined antebellum Tennessee ends in the 1850s as the Whigs splinter internally over the expansion of slavery.
* **Andrew Johnson (Governor, 1853 & 1855):** Democrat elected governor in 1853 and re-elected in 1855; he backs reforms such as public education, wins a **U.S. Senate** seat by the late 1850s, and later sides with the **Union** during the secession crisis.
* **Democratic Dominance:** Democrats keep winning governors' races (1857, 1859), and in the 1856 presidential election Tennessee votes for Democrat **James Buchanan**, its first Democratic presidential vote in this era.
* **Successor Parties:** The **Know-Nothing Party** and an **Opposition Party** emerge from the Whig collapse in Tennessee; nationally the **Republican Party** rises and matters most in the 1860 election.

##### National Crises Over Slavery
* **Compromise of 1850:** Meant to settle questions about slavery's expansion; its most explosive part, the **Fugitive Slave Law**, instead created new tensions.
* **Kansas-Nebraska Act (1854):** Applied **popular sovereignty** (letting settlers decide free or slave status) to the West, igniting violent conflict in Kansas and deepening sectional division, including within Tennessee.
* **Dred Scott Decision (1857):** Controversial Supreme Court ruling that citizenship does not apply to African Americans, deepening the nation's wounds.
* **John Brown's Raid on Harpers Ferry (1859):** An abolitionist's failed attempt to spark an insurrection against slavery by seizing the federal arsenal; Brown's execution made him a martyr to abolitionists and alarmed white Southerners.

##### Tennessee's Divided Position
* **Nashoba Marker Clarification:** The Summer Avenue marker for Fanny Wright's Nashoba (Lecture 19) is not the actual site, which lies near **Germantown** along the **Wolf River**.
* **Tennessee's Unionist Movement:** Tennessee was an unusual Southern state with a real (if relatively small) Unionist movement. Tennesseans, Black and white, fought on both sides of the war.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Slavery's expansion breaks the party system:** Debates over slavery in the West splintered the Whigs and ended Tennessee's two-party rivalry, leaving the Democrats dominant.
2. **Each "solution" deepened the crisis:** The Compromise of 1850, Kansas-Nebraska Act, and Dred Scott decision were each meant to settle the slavery question or to rule on it, and each instead widened the sectional divide.
3. **Johnson's rise:** A decade of electoral success moves Andrew Johnson from governor to U.S. Senator, positioning him as a prominent Southern Unionist.
4. **A divided state:** Tennessee's Unionist minority foreshadows a state whose people will fight on both sides of the Civil War.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This lecture surveys the national and Tennessee politics of the 1850s that set the stage for disunion and the Civil War.

1. **Clarification on Nashoba:** The lecturer first corrects an earlier point: the **Nashoba** marker on **Summer Avenue** is not the colony's actual site, which lay near **Germantown** along the **Wolf River**.
2. **The End of the Whigs & Democratic Dominance (1850s):** The long Whig–Democrat rivalry ended as the **Whig Party** splintered over slavery's expansion. Democrats dominated: **Andrew Johnson** won the governorship in **1853** and **1855**, Democrats won again in 1857 and 1859, and in **1856** Tennessee backed Democrat **James Buchanan** for president. The **Know-Nothings** and an **Opposition Party** arose from the Whig collapse in Tennessee, while the **Republican Party** emerged nationally. Johnson pushed reforms like public education, reached the **U.S. Senate**, and would side with the **Union** during the secession crisis.
3. **National Crises Over Slavery (1850–1857):** The **Compromise of 1850** was meant to settle the slavery question, but its **Fugitive Slave Law** caused new tensions. The **Kansas-Nebraska Act (1854)** and its principle of **popular sovereignty** set off violence in Kansas and divided Tennesseans too. The Supreme Court's **Dred Scott decision (1857)** ruled that citizenship did not apply to African Americans, deepening the divide.
4. **John Brown's Raid (1859):** Abolitionist **John Brown** and his followers raided the federal arsenal at **Harpers Ferry**, hoping to spark an insurrection against slavery. They failed, but Brown's willingness to die for emancipation made him a martyr and intensified sectional hostility.
5. **Tennessee on the Eve of Crisis:** Unlike most Southern states, Tennessee had a real **Unionist movement**. Its people were not united on slavery or secession, and Tennesseans, Black and white, would fight for both the Confederacy and the Union.

### Lecture 23: The Secession Crisis — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### Key Terms & Tennessee's Unique Position
* **Secession / Secede:** The attempt by Southern states to leave the United States and form the **Confederate States of America**; whether states could constitutionally secede was hotly debated.
* **Tennessee's Unique Position:** The **last Southern state to secede** and the **first to formally rejoin** the Union. It had the most significant internal divisions of any Confederate state, and many Tennesseans fought for the Union. *(The lecturer flags Tennessee's unique Civil War characteristics as a likely **final exam** question.)*

##### The 1860 Election
* **Republican Party:** New party committed to a more active federal government (infrastructure, economic policy) and to at least halting slavery's expansion, which terrified slaveholding Southerners.
* **Abraham Lincoln:** Republican nominee who defeats **Stephen Douglas** and the other candidates; he was not even on the ballot in Tennessee and much of the South.
* **John Bell & the Constitutional Union Party:** Tennessean "compromise" candidate stressing sectional reconciliation; Bell wins the most votes in Tennessee but does poorly nationally.
* **Fire-Eaters:** Radical secessionists (especially in **South Carolina**, Virginia, and Alabama) who believed slavery could never be safe in a union with the North and saw Lincoln's election as an apocalyptic threat.
* **Conditional Unionism:** Many white Southerners cherished the Union, but only as long as slavery, which they saw as a fundamental right, was protected.

##### Secession Unfolds (1860–1861)
* **South Carolina Secedes (December 1860):** The first state to leave; by **February 1861** seven states have seceded and formed the Confederacy.
* **Lincoln's First Inaugural Address (March 1861):** Appeals to shared memory and patriotism: "We are not enemies, but friends… the mystic chords of memory… the better angels of our nature."
* **Border States:** Virginia, Tennessee, North Carolina, and Arkansas had not yet seceded; the Confederacy needed them because the seceded states lacked manufacturing and population.
* **Fort Sumter (April 1861):** South Carolina forces fire on the federal garrison, starting the war and pushing conditional Unionists toward the Confederacy.

##### Tennessee's Path to Secession
* **Governor Isham G. Harris:** West Tennessee lawyer, planter, and Democrat who pushed hard for secession.
* **February 1861 Referendum:** Voters decisively reject calling a secession convention; some Unionists voted for a convention hoping it would defeat secession.
* **Regional Divide:** **West Tennessee** (more enslaved people, plantations, cotton) favored secession; **East Tennessee** strongly opposed it; Middle Tennessee shifted after Fort Sumter.
* **Declaration of Independence & June 1861 Vote:** After Sumter, Harris asks the legislature for a declaration of Tennessee independence based on the right of revolution; voters approve, and Tennessee secedes in **June 1861**, the **last state to do so**, with East Tennessee still voting against.
* **Confederate Internal Weakness:** Divided loyalties produced persistent **desertion** and a weak shared Confederate identity, much of which was forged only after the war.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Lincoln's election as the trigger:** The Republicans' anti-expansion stance, amplified by fire-eater rhetoric, turned sectional tension into a secession crisis.
2. **Conditional Unionism:** Many border-state Southerners opposed secession only until war broke out; Fort Sumter shifted their loyalty to their state and region.
3. **Geography of loyalty:** Slavery and cotton made West Tennessee secessionist, while East Tennessee stayed Unionist. That split explains Tennessee's slow path to secession.
4. **Division as a Confederate weakness:** Internal divisions led to desertion and a fragile Confederate identity, which will shape Tennessee's Civil War.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This lecture explains how Lincoln's election set off the secession crisis and why Tennessee, deeply divided, became the last state to leave the Union.

1. **Terms & Tennessee's Uniqueness:** **Secession** refers to Southern states' attempt to leave the U.S. and form the **Confederate States of America**. Tennessee was the **last state to secede** and the **first to formally rejoin**, and it saw the sharpest internal divisions, with many Tennesseans fighting for the Union. The lecturer notes this could be a **final exam** question.
2. **The Election of 1860:** The new **Republican Party** favored an active federal government and at least halting slavery's expansion. **Abraham Lincoln** won the presidency, defeating **Stephen Douglas** and others, though he was not even on Tennessee's ballot. Tennessean **John Bell** of the **Constitutional Union Party** ran on sectional reconciliation and carried Tennessee but fared poorly nationally.
3. **Southern Reaction & the First Secessions (Nov 1860–Mar 1861):** Radical **fire-eaters**, especially in **South Carolina**, argued slavery could never be safe in the Union and treated Lincoln's win as apocalyptic. Many Southerners' love of the Union was **conditional** on protecting slavery. **South Carolina seceded in December 1860**, and seven states formed the Confederacy by **February 1861**. In his **March 1861 inaugural address**, Lincoln appealed to "the better angels of our nature," while the Confederacy desperately needed the more populous, industrial **border states**.
4. **Tennessee Rejects, Then Embraces, Secession (Feb–June 1861):** Governor **Isham G. Harris**, a West Tennessee planter and Democrat, called a special legislative session, but in **February 1861** voters decisively rejected a secession convention. West Tennessee leaned secessionist, and East Tennessee was strongly Unionist. After **Fort Sumter (April 1861)**, conditional Unionists flipped. Harris sought a **declaration of independence** based on the **right of revolution**, and in **June 1861** Tennessee seceded, the last state to do so, with East Tennessee still voting against.
5. **A Divided Confederacy:** Tennessee's divisions mirrored a broader Confederate weakness: persistent **desertion**, weak shared identity, and many people who hated the Union army without being committed Confederates. Many Tennesseans would refuse to fight for the Confederacy.

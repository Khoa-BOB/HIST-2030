# Week 6 Summary & Study Focus

**Big picture:** Week 6 moves from antebellum society to disunion. It traces the 1850s collapse of the Whig Party and the national crises over slavery's expansion, then follows the 1860 election and the secession crisis. It ends with why Tennessee, deeply divided by region, was the last state to secede in June 1861.

### Quick recap
- **Lecture 22 – The Road to Disunion (1850s):** The **Whig Party** splinters over slavery's expansion, and the Know-Nothings, an Opposition Party, and (nationally) the Republicans emerge. **Democrats dominate Tennessee**: Andrew Johnson is governor in 1853 and 1855 and then a U.S. Senator, and Tennessee votes for Buchanan in 1856. Nationally, the Compromise of 1850 (Fugitive Slave Law), the Kansas-Nebraska Act (1854, popular sovereignty), Dred Scott (1857), and John Brown's raid on Harpers Ferry (1859) each deepen the crisis. Tennessee has an unusual **Unionist movement**.
- **Lecture 23 – The Secession Crisis:** Lincoln wins in 1860 without even appearing on Tennessee's ballot; Tennessean **John Bell** (Constitutional Union) carries the state. **Fire-eaters** push secession: South Carolina leaves in Dec 1860, and seven states form the Confederacy by Feb 1861. Lincoln's inaugural appeals to "the better angels of our nature." In Tennessee, Gov. **Isham Harris** pushes secession, but voters **reject** a convention in Feb 1861. After **Fort Sumter (Apr 1861)**, conditional Unionists flip, and Tennessee secedes in **June 1861**, the last state to do so, with East Tennessee still opposed.

### What to focus on
1. **Tennessee's unique Civil War position (flagged as a possible final exam question):** It was the last state to secede and the first to rejoin, the most internally divided Confederate state, and a place where many residents fought for the Union.
2. **Geography of loyalty:** West Tennessee (slavery, cotton, plantations) was secessionist, East Tennessee was Unionist, and Middle Tennessee swung after Fort Sumter. This builds directly on Week 5's regional economic differences.
3. **Conditional Unionism:** Many Southerners loved the Union only while slavery was protected, and war (Fort Sumter) moved their loyalty to their state and region.
4. **Escalation chain of the 1850s:** Compromise of 1850 → Kansas-Nebraska Act → Dred Scott → Harpers Ferry → 1860 election. Each attempt to settle the slavery question made it worse.
5. **Andrew Johnson's rise:** From yeoman-backed governor to U.S. Senator and Southern Unionist. Keep him in mind for the war and Reconstruction.
6. **Anchor dates:** 1850 (Compromise), 1853/1855 (Johnson governor), 1854 (Kansas-Nebraska), 1856 (TN votes Buchanan), 1857 (Dred Scott), 1859 (Harpers Ferry), Nov 1860 (Lincoln elected), Dec 1860 (SC secedes), Feb 1861 (Confederacy formed; TN rejects convention), Mar 1861 (Lincoln's inaugural), Apr 1861 (Fort Sumter; Virginia secedes), June 1861 (Tennessee secedes).

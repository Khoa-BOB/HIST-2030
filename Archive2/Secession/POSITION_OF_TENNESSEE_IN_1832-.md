# Position of Tennessee in 1832–3

**Source:** *Republican Banner* (1837–1875), June 5, 1851, p. 2. ProQuest Historical Newspapers: *The Nashville Tennessean*.

The perils to the Republic at this day are just as imminent as at the fearful period indicated. We hear of men now canvassing for the Legislature in Tennessee who are *openly* for Secession or Disunion. Others are so at heart, but fight “under the rose.” In the neighboring States of Mississippi, Georgia, and Alabama, the question is, openly, Union or Disunion. South Carolina leads the van, as she did in 1832–3. We therefore continue to admonish the people to beware! We continue, at intervals, to show the positions of the people, at the former crisis, in the various counties in Tennessee. ## Henry and Hawkins

On the 25th Dec. 1832, a Union meeting was held at Paris, Henry county, the proceedings of which we find published in the Nashville Republican of Jan. 4, 1833. Col. John W. Cook presided and D. S. Greer acted as Secretary—James Cowan, at whose instance the meeting was called, acting as Marshal of the day. Dr. A. Jackson read the Declaration of Independence. Gen. G. W. Terrell read the Proclamation of the President, which was “received with loud demonstrations of applause.” The following patriotic resolutions, offered by Col. John H. Dunlap, were then “adopted with but one dissenting voice.”

*Whereas*, the citizens of Paris and of Henry county, having assembled in the Court House, and having heard the Declaration of Independence and the Proclamation of the President in relation to South Carolina Nullification read, as American citizens do feel it to be their duty to rally around the standard of the Union—Therefore,

*Resolved*, That while we express our firm reliance on the wisdom and patriotism of the President to parry the insidious blow of disunion, we pledge our honor, our fortunes, and our lives, to sustain all his lawful efforts to bring back to duty and allegiance a wandering and rebellious State.

*Resolved*, That we invite all the citizens of the several counties in this and other States, to unite with the friends of the Union in expressing their opinions on this momentous question. We do this, as we have full faith in the power and effect of popular opinion, and as we believe there is wisdom and virtue enough in the great mass of the people to vindicate and support the principles of the federal Union.

*Resolved*, That our confidence in public sentiment is so great, that we are warranted in the belief that when it is firmly and fairly expressed by the people of the South and West and throughout the Union, the door to all hope to the Nullifiers will be closed and poise the deluded citizens of South Carolina upon their judgment and reflection of the awful consequences into which their leaders are drawing them, and make them retrace their unlawful march to disunion, and thereby save the country from revolution and bloodshed.

The following, offered by *Major W. R. Harris*, was then added to the above:

*Resolved*, That the preservation of the Federal Union, at this portentous crisis, is paramount to all earthly considerations—peaceably if practicable but if force must be resorted to, the end will sanctify the means.

## Meeting at Rogersville

At Rogersville, Hawkins county, on the 7th December, 1832, a Union meeting was held, at which Hon. Samuel Powell presided, William O. Winston acted as Secretary. The following gentlemen were appointed to draft resolutions, and report at a subsequent meeting on the 15th, viz: Cleon Moore, John A. McKinney, Jacob Miller, James Amis, John Cochran, jr., Wm. Trent, Thomas Anderson, Joseph Russell, Gale H. Kyle, Orville Rice, Gabriel McGraw, William Rabb, William Nichols, George Hall, Eldridge Hord, Joseph Smith, Nicholas Fain, Martin Phillips, Jesse Creach, Jas. Johnson, Sam’l Wilson, John A. [illegible], [several names illegible], Joseph Roberts, James Willis, Howel Brewer, John Mitchell, Abraham Hawk, Swimpfield Anderson, Lewis Mitchell, Lewis Click, Benjamin Davis, John Reynolds, Ab Kyle, John Young, James Francisco, Orville Bradley, Dicks Alexander, Stokely D. Mitchell, and Peter Parsons.

His honor Samuel Powell was added to the committee.

On the 15th a general meeting of the citizens of the county took place; the committee reported (through a subcommittee composed of Nicholas Fain, S. D. Mitchell, Samuel Powell, O. Bradley, Dicks Alexander, and John A. M’Kinney,) and with some slight verbal alterations the resolutions were adopted, among which are the following:

*Resolved*, That we solemnly pledge ourselves to support the Government of the United States, in all such measures as may be adopted to preserve the Union, and to check and put down all opposition to the laws of the United States, peaceably if it can be done, but forcibly if no other means can effect it.

*Resolved*, That the people of the United States generally be invited to meet, and express their deep disapprobation of all measures calculated to promote civil war and dissolution of the Union.

*Resolved*, That the proceedings of this meeting be signed by the Chairman and Secretary, and that a copy be sent to the Charleston Courier, the Globe, Richmond Enquirer, Washington Republican, Knoxville Register, and U. S. Telegraph for publication, and that a copy be sent to the President of the United States, and to our Representatives and Senators in Congress; and that the editors of newspapers generally, friendly to the object of this meeting, be requested to publish these proceedings.

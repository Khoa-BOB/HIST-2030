## Summary

### Lecture 39: Women's Suffrage in Tennessee — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### Why the South Was Slow
* **Weak Abolitionist Base:** Suffrage grew from abolitionism nationally. Tennessee had no active abolitionist movement after the 1830s.
* **The Pedestal:** A stronger Southern emphasis on traditional gender roles placed white women on a symbolic pedestal, which made it hard to ask for more rights.
* **Biblical Justifications:** Religious arguments for women's subordination, like pro-slavery arguments before them, were widely accepted.

##### Organizations
* **Women's Clubs and Civic Groups:** By the late 19th century, Black and white women joined clubs for self-improvement and took part in reform, history, and civil rights work.
* **United Daughters of the Confederacy:** A group of white Southern women who memorialized and romanticized the Confederacy.
* **Memphis Suffrage League (1889):** Tennessee's first suffrage organization.
* **Tennessee Equal Rights Association (1897):** The statewide suffrage organization.

##### Key Figures
* **Lide Meriwether:** Founder of the Memphis league and a WCTU member. *[Transcript: "Merriweather," "bloody Merriweather."]*
* **John Vertrees:** A lawyer for railroads, liquor interests, and manufacturers who led the opposition.
* **Governor Albert Roberts:** Called the special session for ratification.
* **President Woodrow Wilson:** An ally of the suffragists.
* **Harry Burn:** The legislator who changed his mind and voted for ratification, saying his mother told him to. *[Transcript: "hair burn."]*

##### Major Events
* **Limited Franchise (1919):** The General Assembly granted women limited voting rights.
* **19th Amendment Ratification (Aug. 1920):** With 35 states done, Tennessee became the decisive **36th**. Suffragists wore **yellow roses** and opponents wore **red roses**.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Public activity revealed limits:** Civic work made women more aware of their legal and political restrictions.
2. **Temperance led to suffrage:** Women saw that the vote could help them pass the reforms they wanted.
3. **Business interests opposed it:** Liquor, railroad, and manufacturing interests feared women voters, for example on child labor laws.
4. **State and national fights converged:** Decades of local organizing met the national amendment in Tennessee in 1920.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This lecture explains why suffrage developed slowly in the South and how Tennessee became the decisive state for the 19th Amendment.

1. **A Slow Start:** Suffrage was tied to **abolitionism**, which had little Southern support. Traditional gender roles, the **pedestal**, and **biblical justifications** further limited the cause.
2. **Women in Public Life:** By the late 19th century, Black and white women were active in clubs, temperance, the **United Daughters of the Confederacy**, and civil rights work. That activity brought attention to their legal and political limits.
3. **Organizing (1889–1919):** **Lide Meriwether** formed the first league in Memphis in **1889**, and the **Tennessee Equal Rights Association** followed in **1897**. Women held teas, balls, essay contests, and luncheons. **John Vertrees** led the opposition on behalf of business interests. In **1919**, the General Assembly granted a **limited franchise**.
4. **The Decisive Vote (August 1920):** With 35 states ratified, suffragists asked Governor **Albert Roberts** for a special session, with allies including **President Wilson**. The measure passed the Senate and then hinged on the House. **Harry Burn** changed his vote, explaining that his mother told him to, and Tennessee became the **36th** state to ratify the **19th Amendment**.

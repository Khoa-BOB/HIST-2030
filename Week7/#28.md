### Lecture 28: The Civil War & Tennessee History — The Battle of Franklin, November 1864 (Battlefield Trust Video) — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### The Campaign
* **John Bell Hood's Tennessee Campaign (Fall 1864):** Hood's attempt to retake **Nashville**. The goal was less to capture a city than to **drag the war into 1865** and wear down the North until it gave up.
* **Lincoln's Re-election (November 1864):** Lincoln won about **75% of the soldier vote** on a promise to fight until the Confederacy was defeated. For Confederate soldiers it signaled a desperate, **"win or die trying"** moment.
* **Spring Hill (November 29, 1864):** Hood nearly cut off **John Schofield's** army from Nashville, but over **20,000** Union soldiers slipped past the Confederates overnight along the **Columbia Turnpike**, one of the great escapes of the war.
* **Harpeth River:** Flooded with its bridges down, it trapped Schofield's army at **Franklin** on **November 30**. Union troops built defensive lines south of town, apparently as a precaution.

##### The Battle (November 30, 1864)
* **The 4 PM Assault:** About **20,000** Confederates (**six divisions, 18 brigades**) attacked a similar number of Union troops across open ground. Some **40,000** soldiers converged on a town of about **750** people.
* **The Breach:** The divisions of **Patrick Cleburne** and **John C. Brown** tore a hole **150–200 yards** wide in the Union center. A Union **counterattack** sealed it, and the killing intensified until about 7:30–9 PM.
* **Carter House & Fountain Branch Carter:** Carter watched his farm of **35 years** torn apart while sheltering **15 family members**. His son **Tod Carter**, a Confederate who had escaped from a POW camp to return to the war, was mortally wounded near home and **died in the family parlor**. The video contrasts a romantic version of this story with a more honest one rooted in slavery.
* **Carnton & the McGavocks:** The plantation and family whose porch visitors romanticize. The video reminds viewers that **enslaved people** there could be "bought, sold, punished, given away."

##### Soldiers' Experience & Memory
* **Walter Rorer (20th Mississippi):** Wrote that he and his comrades were "**noble martyrs**" in a "**holy cause**," a mindset that made the Confederate army dangerous.
* **Trauma After the War:** **Josephus Bond** later took his own life. **Edward Moth** (72nd Illinois) spent years in and out of asylums, compulsively building and tearing down fences. **Isaac Sherwood** became a **pacifist** that night, and **Emerson Opdycke** had never seen the dead lie so thick.
* **Battlefield Preservation:** At the **1964 centennial** only a few sites were preserved: the **monument on the square**, **Winstead Hill** (donated **1946**), the **Carter House** (saved **1951**), and **Carnton**. The video argues against later attempts to **marginalize** Franklin's importance.
* **"A New Birth of Freedom":** Franklin did not kill the Old South, but it helped produce a nation redefined by war, without slavery and with equality newly possible. In the video's words, "we sanitized the war."

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Desperation drove the attack:** Lincoln's re-election meant no negotiated peace, so Hood's men believed they had to win or die. That conviction made the assault so ferocious.
2. **Near-success, then catastrophe:** The breach in the Union center showed how close Hood came, and the Union's quick counterattack turned it into one of the war's bloodiest few hours.
3. **War comes home:** The Carter family's story shows civilians, and a soldier dying yards from his own home, caught in combat.
4. **Memory vs. reality:** Romanticized plantations and a neglected battlefield hid the cost of the war, including slavery and lifelong trauma. The video calls for honest remembrance.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This Battlefield Trust video narrates the **Battle of Franklin (November 30, 1864)** through the soldiers and civilians who lived it and asks how the battle has been remembered.

1. **Preservation & Memory:** At the **1964 centennial**, Franklin preserved only a few sites: the monument on the square, **Winstead Hill (1946)**, the **Carter House (1951)**, and **Carnton**. The town showed little interest in reclaiming more of the battlefield, and later accounts often **marginalized** the battle.
2. **The Stakes (Fall 1864):** **John Bell Hood's** campaign aimed to retake **Nashville** and prolong the war into 1865. **Lincoln's re-election**, with about **75% of the soldier vote**, told Confederates that the North would not give up, so Southern soldiers like **Walter Rorer** saw themselves as "noble martyrs" in a "holy cause."
3. **Spring Hill to Franklin (November 29–30, 1864):** Hood nearly cut off **Schofield** at **Spring Hill**, but more than **20,000** Union troops slipped past overnight on the **Columbia Turnpike**. The flooded **Harpeth River** trapped them at **Franklin**, where they dug in south of town.
4. **The Battle (November 30, 1864):** At **4 PM**, about **20,000** Confederates attacked across open ground. **Cleburne's** and **John C. Brown's** divisions broke the Union center, but a counterattack sealed the breach and the slaughter went on into the evening. **Fountain Branch Carter** watched his farm destroyed, and his son **Tod Carter** was mortally wounded within sight of home and died in the family parlor.
5. **Aftermath & Meaning:** Survivors carried the battle for life: **Josephus Bond's** suicide, **Edward Moth's** years in asylums, **Isaac Sherwood's** pacifism. The video presents Franklin as a moment when a new nation began to emerge, one without slavery and with equality newly possible. It warns against romanticizing places like **Carnton**, where enslaved people suffered, and against a "sanitized" memory of the war.

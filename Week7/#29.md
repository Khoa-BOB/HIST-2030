## Summary

### Lecture 29: Looking Over Jordan — African Americans and the War (NPT Video) — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### Life Under Slavery
* **Enslaved Skilled Labor:** Enslaved people in Nashville were **carpenters, stonecutters, brick makers, and barbers**, and there was no such thing as a white barber in Nashville. About **10,000** enslaved people worked in Middle Tennessee's **iron industry**, running smelters and foundries.
* **Underground Railroad:** About **50,000** enslaved people escaped the South. The North's failure to enforce the **Fugitive Slave Act of 1850** became one of the grievances behind the Civil War.
* **Louis Hughes:** Enslaved southeast of **Memphis** from 1850. He made **five escape attempts** with a **$500 reward** on his head and described being whipped "lash after lash" with rawhide.
* **Religion of Liberation:** Enslaved people turned Christianity into a promise of freedom and identified with **Exodus** and the children of Israel. **Spirituals**, sorrow songs, and call-and-response sustained hope, as in the story of a woman on her way to the auction block who drew strength from hearing "**How Firm a Foundation**."
* **Literacy as Liberation:** Teaching enslaved people to read or write was forbidden, because literacy threatened slavery: a literate person could forge a pass or bill of sale. Black children found ways to learn anyway, and many tied literacy to spiritual and physical freedom.

##### Self-Emancipation in Wartime
* **A War to End Slavery:** Black Tennesseans understood the war as a war to end slavery long before Lincoln did. Lincoln began by calling it a "white man's war."
* **Union Invasion as Answered Prayer:** The fall of **Forts Henry and Donelson (February 1862)** opened the state. **Nashville** became the first state capital reclaimed by the Union, and **Memphis** followed in June. Union armies were hated intruders to Confederates and "answered prayers" to the enslaved.
* **Unplanned Emancipation:** Slavery unraveled haphazardly as people freed themselves. Nobody had a plan, and the army at first sent many back from **Nashville, Memphis, and Clarksville** because it could not house, feed, or treat them.
* **Confiscation Acts (1861, 1862):** The **1861** act allowed seizure of Confederate property, including enslaved people, but was half-heartedly enforced. The **1862** act made it a crime to deny refuge to runaways, which **unleashed a flood** of freedom seekers into **contraband camps**.
* **Emancipation Proclamation (1863):** Did **not apply to Tennessee** or other Union-held areas, because Lincoln did not want to alienate loyal slave states. It did open military service to Black men.

##### Black Soldiers
* **United States Colored Troops (USCT):** About **200,000** Black soldiers and another **200,000** laborers served the Union, and nearly **24,000** freedmen served in Tennessee. They fought to prove they deserved the **ballot box** and the **jury box**.
* **Battle of Nashville (December 1864):** On both days the USCT charged the Confederate right flank as a diversion, attacking so fiercely that the enemy took it for the main assault. Hundreds died at **Peach Orchard Hill**, where bodies lay so thick "you could walk… and never touch the ground." The battle helped dispel the myth that Black men would not make good soldiers.
* **"Bottom Rail on Top":** A Black soldier's reply on finding his former master among Confederate prisoners. It became a symbol of the war's revolutionary reversal.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Resistance before war:** Escape, faith, and secret literacy show that enslaved Tennesseans resisted slavery long before 1861.
2. **The enslaved defined the war:** By fleeing to Union lines, enslaved people turned a war "for the Union" into a war against slavery before federal policy caught up.
3. **Policy followed the people:** The Confiscation Acts and the Emancipation Proclamation responded to self-emancipation as much as they caused it.
4. **Military service as a claim to citizenship:** USCT soldiers fought to earn the ballot and full citizenship, and their sacrifice at Nashville challenged racist assumptions.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This Nashville Public Television documentary segment follows African Americans in Tennessee from slavery through self-emancipation and military service during the Civil War.

1. **Slavery in Tennessee:** Enslaved people did much of Nashville's skilled work as carpenters, stonecutters, brick makers, and barbers, and about **10,000** worked in the state's **iron industry**. Slavery was enforced with brutal punishment, as **Louis Hughes's** account of whipping after his escape attempts shows.
2. **Forms of Resistance:** About **50,000** people escaped the South via the **Underground Railroad**, and Northern non-enforcement of the **Fugitive Slave Act** inflamed sectional tensions. Enslaved people made **Christianity** a religion of liberation through **Exodus** and the **spirituals**, and pursued forbidden **literacy** as a path to freedom.
3. **War and Self-Emancipation (1862):** When **Forts Henry and Donelson** fell and **Nashville** and **Memphis** were occupied, enslaved Tennesseans risked everything to reach Union camps. Slavery unraveled without any plan, and the army often sent refugees back from Nashville, Memphis, and Clarksville.
4. **Confiscation and Emancipation (1861–1863):** The weak **First Confiscation Act (1861)** gave way to the **Second (1862)**, which forbade returning runaways and filled **contraband camps**. The **Emancipation Proclamation (1863)** exempted Tennessee but opened the army to Black men.
5. **Black Soldiers and the Battle of Nashville (1864):** Nearly **24,000** freedmen served in Tennessee. At the **Battle of Nashville** in December 1864, the **USCT** made a ferocious diversionary assault and suffered terrible losses at **Peach Orchard Hill**, which helped end the myth that Black men could not fight. A Black soldier's remark on finding his old master as a prisoner, "**bottom rail on top**," captures the revolution the war produced.

# Week 7 Summary & Study Focus

**Big picture:** Week 7 covers the Civil War in Tennessee, from a deeply divided state joining the Confederacy in 1861 to its near-total Union occupation and the war's end there in December 1864. Alongside the military campaigns, it looks at East Tennessee Unionism, the home front, women's wartime politics, and how enslaved Tennesseans freed themselves and fought for the Union.

### Quick recap
- **Lecture 24 – Divided Tennessee:** Tennessee joins the Confederacy (July 1861). Gov. **Isham Harris** raises a 55,000-man provisional army under **Gideon Pillow**, but planters withhold enslaved labor. **East Tennessee Unionists** hold dueling Gay Street rallies, seek separate statehood, and burn bridges (Nov 1861), which brings hangings and martial law. About 30,000–42,000+ Tennesseans fight for the Union compared with about 120,000 for the Confederacy. **Daniel Ellis** guides thousands to Kentucky.
- **Lecture 25 – Forts Henry & Donelson:** **A. S. Johnston's** line (Cumberland Gap to Columbus, KY) cracks at **Mill Springs** (Jan 1862). **Grant & Foote** take **Fort Henry** and **Fort Donelson** (Feb 1862), and **Nashville** becomes the first Confederate state capital to fall.
- **Lecture 26 – Shiloh (video):** The first great battle (April 6–7, 1862). Confederates strike from **Corinth** to protect key railroads, and A. S. Johnston is killed. Grant trades space for time, holds until **Buell** and **Lew Wallace** arrive, and wins on day two. Rumors that he was drunk nearly sink his career.
- **Lecture 27 – Campaigns of 1862–1863:** **Island No. 10** and **Memphis** (June 1862) fall. **Bragg's** Kentucky campaign fails at **Perryville**, and **Stones River** (Dec 1862–Jan 1863) and the Tullahoma campaign push him to **Chattanooga**. Grant takes **Vicksburg**, **Burnside** takes **Knoxville** (Sept 1863), and **Chickamauga** is a costly Confederate win. **Lookout Mountain** (Nov 1863) leaves nearly all of Tennessee under Union control by the end of 1863.
- **Lecture 28 – Franklin (video):** **Hood** tries to retake Nashville after Lincoln's re-election. After the **Spring Hill** escape (Nov 29), the 4 PM assault at **Franklin** (Nov 30, 1864) breaks the Union center before a counterattack seals it. The **Carter family** and **Tod Carter** are at the center of the story, followed by lifelong trauma for survivors and a critique of "sanitized" memory.
- **Lecture 29 – Looking Over Jordan (video):** Enslaved skilled and iron-industry labor, escape, the religion of liberation, and literacy. Self-emancipation in 1862, the **Confiscation Acts** (1861, 1862), and contraband camps. The **Emancipation Proclamation** exempts Tennessee. About 24,000 freedmen serve, and the **USCT** fights at the **Battle of Nashville** (Peach Orchard Hill). "Bottom rail on top."
- **Lecture 30 – Women & the Home Front (video):** Ladies' Aid Societies and women in teaching, factories, and hospitals. "Rich man's war, poor man's fight" (the **Twenty Negro Law**). Yeoman women petition governors, refugees flee guerrillas (**Elizabeth Avery Meriwether**), and the **1863 food riots** (Richmond, **Mary Jackson**) lead to Confederate welfare for soldiers' wives. Contraband camps, legal marriage, and family reunification follow.
- **Lecture 31 – End of the War:** **Andrew Johnson** is military governor (1862–1865) and cracks down on secessionists. The Emancipation Proclamation exempts Tennessee, but slavery is "dead" in practice: about 276,000 people are freed and about 20,000 Black Tennesseans serve. The **Fort Pillow Massacre** (April 1864; Forrest; 238 of 262 Black soldiers became casualties). The **Battle of Nashville** (Dec 1864) ends the war in Tennessee.

### What to focus on
1. **Tennessee as a divided state:** East Tennessee Unionism (conventions, bridge burners, Union enlistment, guerrillas) against secessionist Middle and West Tennessee. This connects directly to Week 6's geography of loyalty.
2. **Why Shiloh mattered (bonus word):** Railroads, Grant's decision to stay and fight, the unprecedented scale of casualties, and its place in the chain of Union victories.
3. **Military chronology:** Mill Springs → Henry/Donelson → Nashville → Shiloh → Island No. 10/Memphis → Perryville → Stones River → Tullahoma → Chickamauga/Chattanooga → Fort Pillow → Franklin → Nashville.
4. **Emancipation from below:** Self-emancipation, the Confiscation Acts, contraband camps, an Emancipation Proclamation that exempted Tennessee, and Black military service. The lecturer flags this material for the **final exam**.
5. **Women and class on the home front:** Women pushed into public work and politics, class resentment over exemptions, food riots, and Confederate welfare.
6. **Andrew Johnson's role:** Unionist military governor, then vice president. This sets up his presidency in Week 8.
7. **Anchor dates:** Apr 1861 (war begins; Gay Street rallies), July 1861 (TN joins CSA), Nov 1861 (bridge burnings), Jan 1862 (Mill Springs), Feb 1862 (Henry, Donelson, Nashville falls), Apr 6–7 1862 (Shiloh; Island No. 10), June 1862 (Memphis), Oct 1862 (Perryville), Dec 31 1862–Jan 2 1863 (Stones River), Jan 1863 (Emancipation Proclamation), July 1863 (Chattanooga; Vicksburg), Sept 1863 (Knoxville; Chickamauga), Nov 1863 (Lookout Mountain), Apr 1864 (Fort Pillow), Nov 30 1864 (Franklin), Dec 1864 (Nashville), spring 1865 (war ends).

## Summary

### Lecture 25: American Civil War, Part 2 — Forts Henry & Donelson and the Fall of Nashville — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### Confederate Defenses
* **General Albert Sidney Johnston:** Took charge of Tennessee's defenses in **fall 1861**. He saw that the **Tennessee** and **Cumberland Rivers** were the likeliest Union invasion routes.
* **Fort Henry & Fort Donelson:** Confederate forts on the **Tennessee River** (Henry) and the **Cumberland River** (Donelson), about **12 miles apart**. Johnston sped up work on both.
* **Johnston's Defensive Line:** A front running from the **Cumberland Gap** in the east to **Columbus, Kentucky**, on the **Mississippi River**. It reflects Tennessee's value as a **strategic prize**.

##### Union Breakthroughs (1862)
* **Battle of Mill Springs (January 1862):** A Union victory in **Kentucky** that cracked Johnston's line and forced Confederates to fall back toward **Murfreesboro** in Middle Tennessee.
* **Ulysses S. Grant & Andrew Foote:** The Union general and the naval flag officer who led the river campaign. **Gunboats** came up the rivers to support the attacks.
* **Fall of Fort Henry (February 1862):** The first fort to fall.
* **Fall of Fort Donelson (February 1862):** The assault began on **February 13**. The Confederates seemed to push it back at points, but Grant reorganized and Confederate leaders surrendered. **Nathan Bedford Forrest** was among those who escaped. It was a "distinct shock" to the South and the worst Confederate loss to that point.
* **Fall of Nashville (late February 1862):** After Johnston's line collapsed, Nashville was abandoned. It became the **first Confederate state capital** taken by Union troops.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Rivers as highways of invasion:** The Tennessee and Cumberland Rivers made Tennessee vulnerable, and Union gunboats and armies used them to break the Confederate line.
2. **Cracking the line:** Mill Springs, Fort Henry, and Fort Donelson came in quick succession and collapsed Johnston's whole defensive front within weeks.
3. **A symbolic blow:** Losing Nashville, the first Confederate capital to fall, shocked the South and opened Middle Tennessee to Union occupation, though the war still had years to run.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This lecture covers the early-1862 Union campaigns that broke Confederate defenses in Tennessee and led to the fall of Nashville.

1. **Johnston's Defenses (Fall 1861):** **General Albert Sidney Johnston** expected Union troops to invade along the **Tennessee** and **Cumberland Rivers**. He rushed work on **Fort Henry** and **Fort Donelson** and built a defensive front from the **Cumberland Gap** to **Columbus, Kentucky**, which shows how important Tennessee was to both sides.
2. **First Cracks (January 1862):** The Union victory at **Mill Springs, Kentucky**, forced Confederates back into Middle Tennessee around **Murfreesboro**.
3. **The River Forts Fall (February 1862):** **Ulysses S. Grant** and naval officer **Andrew Foote** took **Fort Henry**, then marched 12 miles east and besieged **Fort Donelson** while gunboats came up the Cumberland. The assault began on **February 13**. Grant regrouped after early setbacks, and the Confederates surrendered, though **Nathan Bedford Forrest** escaped. The loss was the worst the Confederacy had yet suffered.
4. **Nashville Falls (Late February 1862):** With Johnston's line collapsed, **Nashville** was abandoned and became the **first Confederate state capital** to fall. The lecturer stresses that the war was far from over and sets up the next topic, the **Battle of Shiloh**, covered by video.

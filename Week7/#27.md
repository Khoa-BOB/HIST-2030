## Summary

### Lecture 27: American Civil War (cont'd), 1862–1863 — Military Campaigns in Tennessee — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### Union Gains in 1862
* **Shiloh as a Turning Point (Bonus Word):** Students should be able to explain **why Shiloh was a turning point**. The lecturer adds "Shiloh" as a **bonus word**.
* **Island No. 10 (April 1862):** A Mississippi River stronghold captured by Union forces. Other river forts fell soon after, giving the Union control of critical points on the **Mississippi River**.
* **Occupation of Memphis (June 1862):** Union troops took Memphis. By mid-1862 the Union largely held **West and Middle Tennessee**, while **East Tennessee** was still undecided.

##### Confederate Counteroffensives
* **Army of Tennessee & Braxton Bragg:** The Confederacy's main western army, created to revive hopes lost at Shiloh. General **Braxton Bragg** took command after Johnston's death.
* **Edmund Kirby Smith & the Kentucky Campaign (1862):** Bragg and Kirby Smith, the East Tennessee commander, launched a northern campaign to regain Tennessee and bring **Kentucky** into the Confederacy. **Nathan Bedford Forrest's** cavalry raids in Middle Tennessee helped Bragg move to **Chattanooga (August 1862)** and then through **Carthage** into central Kentucky.
* **Battle of Perryville (October 1862):** In Kentucky. The campaign failed, and Bragg retreated to East Tennessee and then west to **Murfreesboro**.
* **Battle of Stones River (December 31, 1862 – January 2, 1863):** Bragg's surprise attack on Union troops near **Murfreesboro**. After a lull on January 1, fighting resumed and the Union **repulsed** the attack, and Bragg retreated to **Tullahoma**.

##### Union Control by 1863
* **Tullahoma Campaign (Summer 1863):** Union pressure forced Bragg out of Tullahoma and into **Chattanooga** by **July 1863**, leaving most of Tennessee under Union occupation.
* **Vicksburg (July 1863):** **Grant's** decisive victory on the Mississippi, which set the stage for later Union successes.
* **Ambrose Burnside & Knoxville (August–September 1863):** Burnside's **Army of the Ohio** moved through Kentucky into East Tennessee and entered **Knoxville in September 1863**, welcomed by cheering, long-repressed Unionists.
* **Battle of Chickamauga (September 19–20, 1863):** Fought in northern Georgia south of Chattanooga after the Army of Tennessee evacuated the city. It was a **costly Confederate victory** with heavy losses on both sides. Union troops retreated into **Chattanooga**, and Bragg tried to **cut off its supplies**, causing food shortages.
* **Grant in Command:** Lincoln put **Grant** in command of Union forces in the region in **fall 1863**. By **spring 1864** he commanded all Union armies.
* **Battles for Chattanooga (November 1863):** Battles including **Lookout Mountain** pushed the Confederates back into Georgia. **James Longstreet's Knoxville campaign** also failed. By the **end of 1863** nearly all of Tennessee was under Union control, though Confederate **guerrilla bands** kept fighting.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Rivers, then railroads:** Union control of the Mississippi (Island No. 10, Memphis, Vicksburg) and of Middle Tennessee's rail hubs steadily squeezed the Confederacy.
2. **Every Confederate counteroffensive failed:** The Kentucky campaign (Perryville), Stones River, Chickamauga, and Longstreet's Knoxville campaign either failed outright or won victories too costly to keep.
3. **Chattanooga as the gateway:** The fight shifted to Chattanooga, the gateway to Georgia, and its capture in November 1863 effectively decided Tennessee.
4. **Grant's rise:** From Shiloh to Vicksburg to Chattanooga, Grant's successes led to command of all Union armies by 1864.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This lecture traces the major military campaigns in and around Tennessee from spring 1862 through the end of 1863, by which time nearly the whole state was under Union control.

1. **Union Momentum (Spring–Summer 1862):** **Shiloh** was a decisive turning point (and a **bonus word**). In April 1862 Union forces also captured **Island No. 10**, and other Mississippi River forts soon fell. By **June 1862** Union troops occupied **Memphis**, and the Union held most of **West and Middle Tennessee**. **East Tennessee** was still contested.
2. **Bragg's Kentucky Campaign (Summer–Fall 1862):** The Confederates formed the **Army of Tennessee** under **Braxton Bragg**, who joined **Edmund Kirby Smith** in a campaign to retake Tennessee and win **Kentucky**. **Forrest's** cavalry raids helped Bragg reach **Chattanooga** in **August 1862** and push into Kentucky, but the campaign ended in failure at **Perryville (October 1862)**, and Bragg retreated to **Murfreesboro**.
3. **Stones River to Chattanooga (December 1862 – July 1863):** At **Stones River**, Bragg's surprise attack was repulsed after fighting resumed on January 2, and he fell back to **Tullahoma**. A Union campaign in **summer 1863** drove him into **Chattanooga** by **July**, leaving most of the state occupied. That same month, **Grant** won at **Vicksburg**.
4. **East Tennessee & Chickamauga (Fall 1863):** **Ambrose Burnside** entered **Knoxville** in **September 1863** to cheering Unionists. The Army of Tennessee left Chattanooga and won a **costly victory at Chickamauga (September 19–20)**, then trapped Union troops in Chattanooga and cut off supplies. Lincoln gave **Grant** command in the region. By spring 1864 he led all Union armies.
5. **Tennessee Falls to the Union (November–December 1863):** Battles around Chattanooga, including **Lookout Mountain**, pushed the Confederates into Georgia, and **Longstreet's Knoxville campaign** failed. By the end of **1863** nearly all of Tennessee was under Union control, though Confederate **guerrillas** kept fighting.

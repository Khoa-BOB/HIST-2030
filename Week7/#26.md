## Summary

### Lecture 26: American Civil War, Part 3 — The Battle of Shiloh (Battlefield Trust Video) — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### The Battle
* **Battle of Shiloh (April 6–7, 1862):** The **first big battle** of the Civil War and the largest of the Mississippi Valley campaign. It produced more casualties than there were men in the armies of the **Revolution** or the **War of 1812**, and later battles like Antietam and Gettysburg were "other Shilohs."
* **Pittsburg Landing & Snake Creek Bridge:** Where Union forces camped after arriving in **mid-March 1862**, following Forts Henry and Donelson, and where they were pushed back into an enclave on the first day.
* **Corinth, Mississippi:** Confederate staging point **22 miles southwest**, where the **Memphis & Charleston** and **Mobile & Ohio** railroads crossed. Defending those railroads meant defending the Mississippi Valley.
* **Fraley Field:** Where the first shots were fired at dawn on **April 6, 1862**.
* **Hornet's Nest, Peach Orchard & the Crossroads:** Sites of the heaviest fighting on both days.

##### Commanders
* **Albert Sidney Johnston:** Confederate commander who marched north to destroy Grant's army before Buell arrived. He was **killed** on the first day.
* **P. G. T. Beauregard:** Replaced Johnston and ordered the retreat to Corinth after the second day.
* **Ulysses S. Grant:** **Traded space for time** on day one, knowing reinforcements would arrive by nightfall. His **decision to stay and fight** is where he won the battle.
* **Reinforcements:** **Lew Wallace's** division and **Don Carlos Buell's Army of the Ohio**, which arrived overnight and enabled the Union offensive on **April 7**.
* **Henry W. Halleck:** Grant's commander, who along with the public blamed Grant after Shiloh.

##### Legacy for Grant
* **"In the Doghouse":** Rumors that Grant was drunk at Shiloh put him under a cloud. His **dogged determination** carried him through, and Shiloh became a **badge of honor** on the path to **Vicksburg**, **Chattanooga**, Virginia, and the **White House**.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Railroads drove the strategy:** The Confederate attack came out of the need to protect the rail junction at Corinth and, through it, the Mississippi Valley.
2. **Time beat space:** Grant gave up ground all day on April 6 to hold until reinforcements came, and that choice turned a near-disaster into victory.
3. **A new scale of war:** Shiloh showed Americans a scale of bloodshed they had never seen before and set the pattern for the large battles that followed.
4. **Victory with a cost to reputation:** Shiloh nearly derailed Grant's career before becoming a foundation of it.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This short Battlefield Trust video explains why the **Battle of Shiloh** was a major turning point and what it meant for Ulysses S. Grant.

1. **Setting the Stage (March–April 1862):** After taking Forts Henry and Donelson, Union forces reached **Pittsburg Landing** in **mid-March 1862**. Confederates gathered at **Corinth, Mississippi**, the junction of the **Memphis & Charleston** and **Mobile & Ohio** railroads, and marched north in early April to destroy **Grant's** army before **Buell's Army of the Ohio** could join it.
2. **Day One (April 6, 1862):** Fighting began at dawn in **Fraley Field**. Confederates battered the Union camps and pushed them back through three defensive lines into an enclave around **Pittsburg Landing** and **Snake Creek Bridge**, but lost their commander, **Albert Sidney Johnston**. Grant **traded space for time** and chose to **stay and fight**, the decision the narrator says won the battle.
3. **Day Two (April 7, 1862):** Reinforced by **Lew Wallace** and **Buell**, the Union went on the offensive and drove the Confederates back across the same ground, including the **Hornet's Nest**, **Peach Orchard**, and the crossroads. **Beauregard** withdrew to Corinth.
4. **Significance:** Shiloh was the war's **first great battle**, with more casualties than the armies of the Revolution or the War of 1812 combined. For Grant it brought criticism from **Halleck** and rumors of drunkenness, but he persevered, and Shiloh became a stepping stone toward **Vicksburg**, **Chattanooga**, and the presidency.

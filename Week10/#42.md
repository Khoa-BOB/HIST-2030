### Lecture 42: Tennessee in the 1930s & 1940s — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### The New Deal
* **New Deal Work Relief:** Programs such as the **Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC)**, **Works Progress Administration (WPA)**, and **Public Works Administration** built roads, parks, and schools and provided jobs. In the Jim Crow South, benefits were not evenly shared between white and Black Tennesseans.
* **Rural Electrification Administration (REA):** A federally backed effort to bring power to rural areas that private utilities had skipped as unprofitable.
* **Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA, 1933):** A public power company and regional planning agency. Its dams along the Tennessee River generated cheap hydroelectricity and created reservoirs for recreation, but they also permanently flooded homes and communities. *[Transcript: "TBA," "CVA."]*

##### Politics
* **E. H. Crump:** The "kingmaker" of Tennessee politics, based in Shelby County, and in Congress from **1931 to 1935**. Memphians generally liked him, but rural Tennesseans saw him and Memphis as corrupt and wicked. In the 1930s, he stepped up tactics against Black residents who would not go along with him. *[Transcript: "Trump," "Cramp."]*
* **Poll Tax and Low Turnout:** Fewer than **one-third** of eligible voters took part in the interwar years. Crump's machine paid poll taxes in Shelby County and handed out receipts so people would vote as he wished.
* **Rejuvenated NAACP:** A younger generation in Memphis that was less willing to go along with the Crump machine.
* **Gordon Browning:** A World War I veteran elected governor after announcing his run in **1936**, relatively independent of Crump. He reorganized state government, increased school funding, and raised business taxes to address state debt. His challenge to Crump in Shelby County backfired.

##### World War II
* **Clinton Engineer Works (Oak Ridge):** The "secret city" and a key **Manhattan Project** site, bringing tens of thousands of people. The lecture says it became **Oak Ridge National Laboratory**. *[Correction: ORNL grew out of Clinton Laboratories, the lab within the site, renamed in 1948.]*
* **Wartime Industry:** Limited compared with other states. The **Vultee** aircraft factory in Nashville built dive bombers. *[Transcript: "Volti."]*
* **Camp Forrest:** A U.S. Army training camp at **Tullahoma**, among the largest in the country. From **1942** it also held about **22,000** prisoners of war and internees, including Japanese Americans. *[Transcript: "camp Forest," "until Oklahoma." Unconfirmed: I could not confirm the 22,000 figure.]*
* **Other Home Front Activity:** Army maneuvers, rationing, rubber salvage drives, and Camps Campbell and Crossville.

##### Postwar Effects
* **Federal Dollars and Universities:** Oak Ridge research and the nuclear industry benefited Tennessee's universities, which expanded and became more research-focused in the **1940s and 1950s**, including the University of Tennessee, Knoxville, and Memphis State (later the University of Memphis).
* **G.I. Bill:** Gave returning veterans access to a college education that many could not otherwise afford.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Federal money as the engine:** The New Deal, TVA, and wartime spending drove rapid change in Tennessee, especially the Tennessee Valley.
2. **Progress with costs:** TVA brought light and cheap power but displaced people, and relief programs were uneven under Jim Crow.
3. **Machine politics and low turnout:** The poll tax kept most people from voting, which gave a machine like Crump's its leverage.
4. **Urban-rural divide:** Crump's diverse, more cosmopolitan Memphis became a symbol of that friction, which persists today.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This lecture covers the Depression, the New Deal, Crump-era politics, and World War II, which together transformed Tennessee.

1. **The New Deal (1933–):** After **Franklin Roosevelt's** election, work relief programs such as the **CCC** and **WPA** brought federal dollars and visible improvements, though they were unequal under **Jim Crow**. Rural Southerners often lacked electricity because private utilities would not run lines to them, so the **REA** stepped in.
2. **TVA:** Created in **1933**, the **TVA** was both a public power company and a planning agency. Its dams generated hydroelectricity and provided recreation, but flooded homes and left bitterness among those who had to leave. Like the Smokies park, it had costs as well as benefits. It also shaped what came in the 1940s.
3. **Crump and Browning (1930s):** **Crump** stayed the "kingmaker" and served in Congress from **1931 to 1935**. Under the **poll tax**, fewer than one-third of eligible voters took part. Rural Tennessee distrusted him and Memphis, a diverse city of Catholic, Jewish, and voting Black residents. **Gordon Browning** reorganized government and raised school funding, but his effort to oversee Shelby County voting backfired, and Crump won the court fight. Crump also grew more vindictive toward Black residents who defied him, including younger **NAACP** activists.
4. **World War II:** **Clinton Engineer Works** at Oak Ridge was a pivotal **Manhattan Project** site. Industry was limited, but **Vultee** built dive bombers in Nashville. **Camp Forrest** trained soldiers and held prisoners and internees, including Japanese Americans. Civilians rationed and salvaged rubber.
5. **Long-Term Legacy:** Oak Ridge became **Oak Ridge National Laboratory**. *[Correction: through Clinton Laboratories, renamed in 1948.]* It brought federal money and researchers, which helped Tennessee's universities grow. Veterans used the **G.I. Bill** to attend college. Along with TVA and the park, these changes opened a new era for the state.

### Lecture 40: Tennessee in the 1920s — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### Politics & Reform
* **Austin Peay:** Governor elected in **1922, 1924, and 1926**, the first to win three elections since the Civil War. A classic progressive focused on efficiency, he improved roads and rural schools, modernized state government, and balanced the budget. He died in **1927** from complications of influenza. *[Transcript: "Austin P." Correction: sources give a cerebral hemorrhage as the cause of death.]*
* **Rural Base:** Peay's first base was urban, but taxing cities to fund rural improvements cost him city support while winning over rural voters.

##### Music & Modern Culture
* **Modern American Culture:** Tennesseans embraced film, music, consumer goods, and radio, and helped create this culture.
* **Blues and W. C. Handy:** Handy, connected to Memphis, helped popularize the blues.
* **Country ("Hillbilly") Music:** Nashville became its center.
* **WSM:** The radio station of Nashville's **National Life and Accident Insurance Company**, founded in **1925**. Its initials came from the motto "We Shield Millions."
* **George D. Hay:** An Indiana native and former Memphis news reporter who started the nation's first country radio show, Chicago's **National Barn Dance**, then brought the model to WSM. *[Correction: Hay was an announcer on the WLS National Barn Dance, not its creator.]*
* **Grand Ole Opry:** The WSM Saturday night barn dance, renamed in **1927**. It helped take country music national. *[Correction: the lecture can be heard as saying the barn dance began in 1927; it debuted on November 28, 1925, and only the renaming was in 1927.]*

##### Cultural Tension
* **The New Woman and African American Artists:** The flapper, the Harlem Renaissance, blues, and jazz showed a culture changing nationally.
* **Second Ku Klux Klan:** It re-emerged as a national organization, a darker rejection of modern culture. It had less presence in Tennessee, partly because leaders like **Crump** rejected its racism and anti-Catholicism.

##### The Scopes Trial
* **Butler Act (March 1925):** Sponsored by **John Washington Butler** of Macon County, a farmer and Primitive Baptist, it banned teaching evolution in state schools. It passed easily, Peay signed it, and it stayed on the books until **1967**.
* **ACLU:** Founded in 1920, it offered to fund a test case.
* **John T. Scopes:** The 22-year-old teacher who agreed to be the defendant after a drugstore debate in **Dayton**. *[Correction: Scopes was born on August 3, 1900, so he was 24 during the trial.]*
* **Clarence Darrow and William Jennings Bryan:** The defense attorney and the volunteer prosecutor assistant.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Progressive modernization:** Peay's roads, schools, and efficient government opened up isolated rural areas.
2. **Tennessee as a culture creator:** Tennesseans did not merely adopt modern culture, since blues and country helped define it.

4. **Myth vs. reality:** Dayton sought publicity and the penalty was a small fine, unlike the popular image of a threatened teacher.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This lecture highlights three dimensions of the 1920s: Austin Peay's government, Tennessee's role in modern culture, and the Scopes Trial.

1. **Governor Austin Peay (1923–1927):** After falling short of the nomination in **1918**, **Peay** won in **1922, 1924, and 1926**. Historians rank him among Tennessee's best governors for his **roads**, **rural schools**, and modernized government. He balanced the budget, but taxes on cities cost him urban support. He died in **1927** of complications from influenza. *[Correction: sources say a cerebral hemorrhage.]*
2. **A Musical Decade:** **Blues** and **country** became part of national culture. **W. C. Handy** helped popularize the blues. In **1925**, National Life created **WSM** and hired **George D.

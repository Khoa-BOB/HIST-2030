# Week 10 Summary & Study Focus

**Big picture:** Week 10 covers Tennessee from the 1920s through World War II. It moves from Austin Peay's reforms, country music, and the Scopes Trial to the Great Smoky Mountains National Park, the New Deal and TVA, Crump-era politics, and Oak Ridge.

### Quick recap
- **Lecture 40 – The 1920s:** **Austin Peay** (elected 1922, 1924, 1926) modernized roads, rural schools, and government, and died in 1927. *[Correction: the lecture says influenza complications; sources say a cerebral hemorrhage.]* **W. C. Handy** and blues, plus **WSM** (1925) and the **Grand Ole Opry** (named 1927), shaped national culture. The **Butler Act (March 1925)** led to the **Scopes Trial (July 1925)**, with **Darrow** against **Bryan**. Scopes was convicted, the conviction was overturned, and the law lasted until **1967**. *[Corrections: Scopes was 24, not 22; the Tennessee Supreme Court overturned the conviction; Bryan died five days after the trial, not a week.]*
- **Lecture 41 – Great Smoky Mountains National Park:** Cherokee and settler practices shaped the Smokies, then logging clearcut **60%** in 20 years. Advocates like **Ambler**, **Kephart**, and **Broome**, along with **Anne and Willis Davis**, pushed for a park. Knoxville, schoolchildren, the states, Rockefeller money, and federal funds bought the land. **FDR** dedicated it in **September 1940**, and most residents had to leave. *[Corrections: the Smokies lie in Tennessee and North Carolina, not South Carolina; the donor was the Laura Spelman Rockefeller Memorial.]*
- **Lecture 42 – The 1930s & 1940s:** The **New Deal** brought the **CCC**, **WPA**, and **REA**, while the **TVA (1933)** brought dams and cheap power along with flooded communities. **Crump** stayed the "kingmaker" and defeated **Gordon Browning's** challenge. Under the poll tax, fewer than one-third of eligible voters took part. In **World War II**, Tennessee had **Oak Ridge**, **Vultee**, and **Camp Forrest**. Afterward, **ORNL** and the **G.I. Bill** helped universities grow. *[Correction: ORNL grew out of Clinton Laboratories, not directly from the whole Clinton Engineer Works.]*

### What to focus on
1. **Urban-rural tension:** This runs through Peay's taxes, the Scopes coverage, and the distrust of Crump's Memphis. It also continues Week 9's wets vs. dries.
2. **Modernity vs. tradition:** Radio and the Opry existed alongside the Klan and the Butler Act. Know the difference between the Scopes myth and the reality.
3. **Progress and its costs:** Both the Smokies park and TVA brought major benefits but displaced people from their homes.
4. **Development vs. preservation:** The lumber industry, the park, and tourism illustrate the old Southern dilemma.
5. **Federal power changes Tennessee:** The New Deal, TVA, the Smokies funding, and Oak Ridge brought federal dollars. This connects to your Oak Ridge reading on the secret city.
6. **Anchor dates:** 1899 (Appalachian National Park Association), 1922 (Peay elected), 1925 (Butler Act; Scopes; WSM), 1927 (Opry named; Peay dies), 1933 (TVA; FDR inaugurated), 1936 (Browning's run), 1940 (park dedicated), 1942 (Camp Forrest holds prisoners), 1967 (Butler Act ends).

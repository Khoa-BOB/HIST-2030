## Summary

### Lecture 17: Antebellum Tennessee Economy & Society — Population, Cotton & Landholding — Entities, Relationships & Summary

#### 1. Key Keyword Entities in Tennessee History

##### Population & Urbanization
* **Antebellum Population Growth:** Driven largely by migration from eastern states, Tennessee's population grows roughly **162%** in the decades before the Civil War, with most of that growth in **Middle and West Tennessee**.
* **Memphis & Nashville:** The two big cities that emerge as major centers of population and activity in antebellum Tennessee.
* **Secondary Towns:** **Knoxville, Chattanooga, Columbia, Murfreesboro, and Clarksville** also grow during this period.
* **Rural Character:** Despite urbanization, Tennessee remains overwhelmingly **rural** — most people live outside population centers and are tied to agriculture.

##### Agricultural Economy
* **Tobacco & Livestock:** Staple agricultural pursuits of Tennessee farmers alongside general farming.
* **Cotton Boom:** The defining agricultural development of the antebellum era; cotton production booms dramatically and comes to characterize the economy of **West Tennessee**, mirroring trends in the Mississippi Delta, Georgia, and Alabama.

##### Landholding & Labor
* **Plantation (definition):** A farm with at least **20 enslaved people** working on it; Tennessee has roughly **3,000 plantations by 1860**, some with hundreds of enslaved laborers.
* **Elite Planter Class:** A small group owning large properties and many enslaved people.
* **Small-to-Medium Farms:** The more typical Tennessee farm was **50–100 acres**, worked by a family, perhaps a hired laborer or two, and sometimes one or two enslaved people — especially characteristic of East Tennessee.

##### Key Dynamics & Evolutionary Drivers
1. **Migration fuels growth:** Eastern migrants drive Tennessee's population boom, concentrating growth in Middle and West Tennessee and producing new urban centers.
2. **Cotton reshapes the West:** The cotton boom ties West Tennessee to the Deep South's plantation economy and to large-scale enslaved labor.
3. **Myth vs. reality of the plantation South:** Plantations existed (~3,000 of them), but most Tennessee farms were modest, with varied mixes of family, hired, and enslaved labor.

#### 3. Lecture Summary

This short lecture sketches the population, economy, and landholding patterns of antebellum Tennessee.

1. **Population Growth & Urbanization (antebellum decades):** Migration from eastern states drove a roughly **162%** population increase, concentrated in **Middle and West Tennessee**. **Memphis** and **Nashville** became the state's two big cities, while **Knoxville, Chattanooga, Columbia, Murfreesboro, and Clarksville** also grew. Even so, Tennessee remained mostly **rural**, with most people living outside these centers.
2. **An Agricultural Economy & the Cotton Boom:** The state economy rested on agriculture — **tobacco**, **livestock**, and above all **cotton**, whose production boomed and came to define **West Tennessee**, much like the Delta and parts of Georgia and Alabama.
3. **Plantations vs. Typical Farms (by 1860):** Tennessee had about **3,000 plantations** (farms with at least **20 enslaved people**), and an elite planter class could hold hundreds of enslaved people. But the typical farm was a modest **50–100 acres**, worked by a family, hired laborers, and sometimes one or two enslaved people. The popular image of the plantation South therefore obscures the wide variety of landholdings and labor arrangements across Tennessee's three grand divisions.
